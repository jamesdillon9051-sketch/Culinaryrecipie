Chana Masala

A punchy North Indian chickpea curry, sour with amchur and tamarind and darkened by a long-fried onion base. Naturally vegan, extremely cheap, and one of the best things you can make from a tin.

Why This Recipe Works

Chana masala is defined by sourness layered from three sources — tomato, dried mango powder and tamarind — each contributing a different acid with a different flavour profile. Mashing a quarter of the chickpeas releases starch that thickens the gravy so it clings rather than sitting as a thin liquid.

Method

Tap a step to highlight it, or turn on Cook Mode for large type and step-by-step focus.

  1. Heat the oil and pop the cumin seeds for 20 seconds until fragrant.

  2. Add the onions and fry over medium heat for 12 to 15 minutes, stirring regularly, until deeply browned. This is the flavour base and cannot be shortened.

  3. Add the garlic, ginger and green chillies and cook 2 minutes.

  4. Stir in the ground coriander, cumin, turmeric and Kashmiri chilli and fry for 45 seconds.

  5. Add the tomatoes and salt and cook for 8 to 10 minutes, mashing them down, until the mixture darkens and the oil separates at the edges.

  6. Add the chickpeas, chana masala powder, amchur, tamarind and water. Simmer for 15 minutes.

  7. Mash about a quarter of the chickpeas against the side of the pan with a spoon and stir them back in to thicken the gravy.

  8. Simmer 5 minutes more, then stir in the garam masala and half the coriander.

  9. Serve topped with sliced red onion, the remaining coriander and a squeeze of lemon.

Chef’s Tips

  • Dried chickpeas soaked overnight with a pinch of bicarbonate of soda give a superior texture — cook them for 45 minutes before starting.
  • A black tea bag added to the cooking water darkens the gravy the traditional way.
  • Amchur is what makes this taste like a proper chana masala. Extra lemon juice is a passable substitute.

Pairing Suggestions

  • A cold lager or salted lassi
  • Serve with bhature, naan or plain rice
  • Finish with fresh mango

Storage & Reheating

Refrigerate 5 days, freeze 3 months. It thickens as it sits; loosen with a splash of water when reheating.
