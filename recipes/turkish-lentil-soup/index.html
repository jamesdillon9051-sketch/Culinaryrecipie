Turkish Lentil Soup

Red lentils cooked to nothing with onion, carrot and potato, blended smooth and finished with a spoonful of chilli butter and a lot of lemon.

Why This Recipe Works

The soup itself is deliberately plain — the flavour arrives at the end with the chilli butter and the lemon, which is why a version tasted before those go in always seems disappointing. Blending it completely smooth is not optional in Turkey; texture is what separates mercimek çorbası from a lentil stew.

Method

Tap a step to highlight it, or turn on Cook Mode for large type and step-by-step focus.

  1. Warm the olive oil in a large pot and cook the onion for 8 minutes until soft and translucent.

  2. Add the carrot and potato and cook 5 minutes more.

  3. Stir in the tomato purée and cumin and cook 2 minutes.

  4. Add the lentils and the stock, bring to a boil, then simmer 30 minutes until the lentils have completely collapsed and the vegetables are very soft.

  5. Blend until absolutely smooth — a stick blender for 3 full minutes, or a jug blender in batches. It should be silky, not grainy.

  6. Return to the pan, add the salt, and thin with a little hot water if it has gone too thick. It should coat a spoon lightly.

  7. For the chilli butter, melt the butter and cook until it just starts to foam, then take it off the heat and stir in the Aleppo pepper and dried mint. It will sizzle and turn red.

  8. Ladle the soup into bowls and spoon the chilli butter over the surface.

  9. Serve with lemon wedges and bread, and squeeze the lemon in generously.

Chef’s Tips

  • Blend for longer than you think. Silky, not grainy.
  • The chilli butter and the lemon are the recipe, not garnishes.
  • Rinse the lentils until the water is clear or the soup will taste dusty.

Pairing Suggestions

  • Lemon wedges
  • Warm flatbread
  • Pickled vegetables

Storage & Reheating

Refrigerate up to 4 days and freeze 3 months. It thickens; loosen with water on reheating.
