Huevos Rotos

Fried eggs broken over golden fried potatoes and slices of Spanish ham, so the yolk runs through everything.

How to Cook Huevos Rotos

Tap a step to highlight it, or turn on Cook Mode for large type and step-by-step focus.

  1. Heat the oil in a wide frying pan over a medium heat. Add the potatoes and cook for 15 minutes, turning, until golden and tender.

  2. Lift out onto a warm plate, drain on kitchen paper and season with half the salt. Top with the ham.

  3. Pour off all but 3 tablespoons of the oil. Fry the eggs over a medium-high heat for 2 minutes, spooning the oil over the whites, until the edges are crisp and the yolks runny.

  4. Slide the eggs over the potatoes, sprinkle with the remaining salt and the paprika, and serve at once. Break the yolks at the table.

Things to Watch For

  1. Use floury potatoes.
  2. Keep the oil at a gentle heat.
  3. Serve at once.

A Little Background

The name means broken eggs, and the breaking is the point. This is a dish found in every bar from Madrid to the south, a pile of golden potatoes with eggs on top, which you crush at the table so that the yolks coat every chip. It is simple, and the only difficulty is getting the potatoes right.

The Spanish method is to cook the potatoes in a lot of olive oil, in a shallow pool, over a medium heat. Slice floury potatoes thin, about 5 mm, and cook for 15 minutes, turning, until they are golden outside and soft inside. They should not be too crisp, because they will absorb the egg. Don't use too hot an oil. The potatoes would brown before they cooked through.

Lift the potatoes out onto a warm plate, scatter with sea salt, and top with slices of jamón serrano. In the same oil, fry the eggs. Spoon the hot oil over the whites to set the tops, and cook for 2 minutes until the edges are lacy and brown, with a runny yolk.

Slide the eggs onto the potatoes and bring to the table. Break the yolks with a fork, mix, and eat at once with bread.
