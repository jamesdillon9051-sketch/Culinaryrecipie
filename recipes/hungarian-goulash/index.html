Hungarian Goulash

A true gulyás is a soup, not a stew: beef, onions and a great deal of sweet Hungarian paprika simmered with potatoes and caraway. The paprika goes in off the heat or the whole pot turns bitter.

Why This Recipe Works

Paprika is rich in sugars and carotenoids that scorch above about 120°C, turning acrid within seconds — which is why the pot comes off the heat before it goes in. An equal weight of onions to beef is not a typo: they melt entirely into the broth and are the only thickener the dish uses.

Method

Tap a step to highlight it, or turn on Cook Mode for large type and step-by-step focus.

  1. Melt the lard in a heavy pot and cook the onions over medium-low heat for 25 to 30 minutes, stirring often, until completely collapsed and golden. This is the base of the dish and it cannot be rushed.

  2. Add the garlic, caraway and green peppers and cook 4 minutes.

  3. Take the pot off the heat entirely and let it cool for 60 seconds.

  4. Stir in both paprikas and 3 tablespoons of water, mixing quickly and thoroughly so the paprika cannot catch.

  5. Return to low heat, add the beef and salt, and stir to coat. Cook for 10 minutes, letting the meat release its juices.

  6. Add the tomatoes, bay leaves and enough stock to cover by 3 cm. Bring to a bare simmer.

  7. Cover and cook gently for 90 minutes, until the beef is beginning to yield.

  8. Add the potatoes and simmer uncovered for 30 minutes more, until they are tender and the broth has thickened slightly.

  9. Stir in the vinegar, rest 15 minutes off the heat, and serve in deep bowls with bread.

Chef’s Tips

  • Buy genuine Hungarian paprika. Generic paprika is a completely different, much duller ingredient.
  • Off the heat before the paprika goes in. Ten seconds of direct heat will ruin the entire pot.
  • Equal weights of onion and beef sounds like a lot. It is correct.

Pairing Suggestions

  • A Hungarian Kékfrankos or a dark lager
  • Serve with crusty bread and pickled peppers
  • Finish with a strudel

Storage & Reheating

Refrigerate 4 days, freeze 3 months. Like most braises, it is better on day two.
