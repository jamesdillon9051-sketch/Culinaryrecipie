Sloppy Joes

Beef mince cooked down in a sweet-sharp tomato sauce and spooned onto a soft bun, engineered to be eaten with both hands and several napkins.

★★★★★★★★★★4.6(1,866)Rated 4.6 out of 5 from 1866 reviews 35 min total Serves 6 servings Easy

Why This Recipe Works

The sauce needs both sugar and vinegar in balance — the sweetness alone reads as tinned spaghetti, the vinegar alone as a bad barbecue sauce. Cooking the mince until the water has gone and the meat is actually browning, rather than stewing grey, is what gives the sandwich savour instead of just sweetness.

Method

Tap a step to highlight it, or turn on Cook Mode for large type and step-by-step focus.

  1. Heat the oil in a wide pan over high heat. Add the mince in one layer and leave it undisturbed for 4 minutes to take colour, then break it up and cook until the liquid has evaporated and it sizzles rather than bubbles.

  2. Push the meat aside, add the onion and pepper, and cook 6 minutes until softened.

  3. Add the garlic for 1 minute, then the tomato purée and cook 2 minutes until it darkens.

  4. Add the passata, vinegar, sugar, Worcestershire, mustard, paprika, salt, pepper and stock.

  5. Simmer 15 minutes uncovered, stirring now and then, until thick enough to mound on a spoon.

  6. Taste and adjust — a little more vinegar if it is flat, a little more sugar if it is sharp.

  7. Spoon generously into toasted buttered buns and serve immediately with pickles.

Chef’s Tips

  • Brown the mince properly. Grey mince makes a sweet sandwich with no depth.
  • Thick enough to sit on a spoon, or it runs straight out of the bun.
  • Toast and butter the buns — it waterproofs them for a minute or two longer.

Pairing Suggestions

  • Dill pickles
  • Crisps or fries
  • A cold lager

Storage & Reheating

The filling refrigerates 4 days and freezes 3 months. Reheat with a splash of stock.
