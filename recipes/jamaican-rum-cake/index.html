Jamaican Rum Cake

A dense Christmas cake of dried fruit soaked in rum and wine, baked, then drenched in rum syrup while still warm.

It tastes of rum, port and burnt sugar, and it is deliberately dark. The Jamaican Christmas cake, also called black cake, starts with dried fruit chopped finely and soaked for weeks in white rum and port wine. The fruit is then puréed into a dark paste, giving the cake its colour, its moisture and its deep flavour.

A full recipe requires a three-week soak, but a fast one can be made by simmering the fruit with the rum and wine for 15 minutes, then leaving it overnight. Blend the soaked fruit to a rough purée. It disperses through the batter and gives the cake its dense, even crumb.

The batter is built on butter and brown sugar creamed for 5 minutes, with eggs, flour, baking powder, mixed spice and a spoonful of burnt sugar syrup (browning) for colour. Fold in the fruit purée and a splash of rum.

Bake in a lined tin at 160°C / 320°F for 1 hour, until a skewer comes out clean. Pierce the top while warm and brush generously with rum. Wrap tightly and leave for a day or two before cutting; the flavour deepens.

How long does Jamaican Rum Cake take to make?

25 minutes of preparation and 1 hour of cooking, plus 24 hours of resting before slicing, so allow 25 hours 25 minutes from start to finish. The method also needs time for cooling the fruit, which it does not time, so treat that as a minimum. The recipe serves 12.

The Method for Jamaican Rum Cake

Before you begin

  • Plan for 24 hr of resting before slicing. It is part of the 25 hr 25 min start to finish.
  • The method also needs time for cooling the fruit, which it does not give, so allow extra.
  • You will need: oven, skewers (20 cm round tin).

Tap a step to highlight it, or turn on Cook Mode for large type and step-by-step focus.

  1. Simmer the raisins, currants, cherries and peel with the 150 ml rum and the port for 15 minutes. Cool, then blend to a rough purée.

  2. Heat the oven to 160°C / 320°F (140°C / 285°F fan) and line a 20 cm round tin.

  3. Beat the butter and sugar for 5 minutes until pale. Beat in the eggs one at a time.

  4. Fold in the flour, baking powder and mixed spice, then the fruit purée, browning and vanilla.

  5. Bake for 60 minutes until a skewer comes out clean. Prick the top and brush with the 4 tbsp rum. Cool, wrap and leave for a day before slicing.
