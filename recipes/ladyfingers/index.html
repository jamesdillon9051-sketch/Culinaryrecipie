Ladyfingers

Light, dry, crisp sponge fingers piped from a whisked egg batter and dusted with icing sugar. The savoiardi for tiramisu, trifles and dipping in coffee.

Ladyfingers: Step by Step

Tap a step to highlight it, or turn on Cook Mode for large type and step-by-step focus.

  1. Heat the oven to 190°C / 375°F (170°C / 340°F fan). Line 2 baking trays with baking parchment and draw parallel lines 8 cm apart on the underside of each sheet, then turn it over.

  2. Beat the egg yolks with 30 g of the sugar and the vanilla for 4 minutes, until pale, thick and doubled in volume.

  3. In a clean bowl, whisk the egg whites with the salt to soft peaks. Add the remaining 60 g sugar a spoonful at a time and whisk to stiff, glossy peaks.

  4. Fold a third of the whites into the yolk mixture to loosen it. Sift over half the flour and fold it in gently, add half the remaining whites, then the last of the flour and whites, folding just until there are no streaks.

  5. Spoon the batter into a piping bag with a 1.5 cm plain nozzle. Pipe 10 cm fingers between the lines, 3 cm apart. Dust generously with half the icing sugar, wait 5 minutes, and dust again with the rest.

  6. Bake for 10 to 12 minutes, until pale golden and firm. Cool on the trays for 5 minutes, then peel off the paper and cool completely on a wire rack.

Keep an Eye on These

  1. Make sure the bowl and whisk are completely clean and dry. A trace of egg yolk or grease in the whites will stop them from whipping.
  2. Fold with a large spatula in wide, slow strokes and stop the moment the streaks vanish. Every extra stroke knocks air out of the batter.
  3. Dust twice with icing sugar. The first layer dissolves into the batter and the second one forms the fine crackled crust.

Good to Know About Ladyfingers

Ladyfingers, or savoiardi in Italian, are the long, light, dry sponge biscuits that are dipped in coffee for tiramisu, that line the mould of a charlotte and that give a trifle its base. They are named after the House of Savoy, and the story is that they were first made at the court of the Duke of Savoy in the fifteenth century for a royal visit. They are made from only eggs, sugar and flour, with no butter or raising agent, and all their lightness comes from the eggs, which are separated and whisked to a foam. The yolks are beaten with sugar until pale and thick, the whites are whipped with more sugar to stiff peaks, and the two are folded together with sifted flour with as little stirring as possible. The batter is piped into fingers, dusted with icing sugar twice, which gives them their characteristic slightly crackled and crisp crust, and baked until they are pale gold. The moment of folding is the critical one. If the batter is over-mixed the foam collapses and the fingers spread flat, and if it is under-mixed there are streaks of white in it. Fold with a large spatula and stop as soon as there are no streaks. Homemade ladyfingers are fresher and lighter than shop-bought ones, and they can be dried in a low oven if they are to be soaked for a dessert.

Keeping Ladyfingers

Keep in an airtight container for up to 2 weeks. For tiramisu, dry them for 10 minutes in a 120°C / 250°F (100°C fan) oven. Freeze for 3 months.
