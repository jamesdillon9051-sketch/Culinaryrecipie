Montreal Smoked Meat Sandwich

Brisket cured for five days, crusted with cracked pepper and coriander, smoked, then finished tender and sliced thick onto rye with mustard. The Montreal deli sandwich, made at home.

The Idea Behind Montreal Smoked Meat Sandwich

Montreal smoked meat is brisket cured in salt and spice, crusted with cracked pepper and coriander, smoked, and then cooked until it will pull apart with a fork. Nearly all of its flavour comes from the five days in the brine, before any heat is used: the cure carries salt and spice to the centre of the meat and turns it pink. The crust builds a dark bark in the smoker, and finishing the meat wrapped with a little water turns its collagen into gelatine, which is what makes the slices soft and juicy rather than dry. The sandwich is the one Montreal's Jewish delicatessens are known for: thick slices, cut by hand against the grain, on rye with plain yellow mustard.

How to Make Montreal Smoked Meat Sandwich

Tap a step to highlight it, or turn on Cook Mode for large type and step-by-step focus.

  1. Make the brine: bring 500 ml of the water to a simmer with the salt, sugar, curing salt, garlic, pickling spice and 1 tablespoon of peppercorns, stirring until the salt and sugar dissolve. Add the remaining 2 litres of cold water and stir. The brine must be completely cold before the meat goes in.

  2. Trim the brisket, leaving a layer of fat about 5 mm thick on one side. Put it in a large non-reactive container or a food-safe bag, pour over the cold brine so the meat is fully covered, and weigh it down with a plate.

  3. Refrigerate for 5 days, turning the meat over once a day.

  4. Lift the brisket out of the brine, rinse it well under cold water and pat it completely dry with paper towels. Throw the brine away.

  5. Make the crust: crack the 3 tablespoons of peppercorns and the coriander coarsely, in a mortar or under the flat of a knife, so they are in pieces and not powder. Stir in the garlic powder, paprika, brown sugar and mustard seeds, then press the mixture firmly over every side of the meat.

  6. Set up a smoker, or a covered barbecue for indirect heat, at 120°C / 250°F and add a few chunks of oak or hickory. Smoke the brisket fat side up for 4 hours, until the crust is dark and the inside reads 85°C.

  7. Heat the oven to 150°C / 300°F. Wrap the brisket tightly in two layers of foil with 120 ml of water and cook for 2 hours more, until a skewer slides in with almost no resistance and the meat reads 95°C / 205°F.

  8. Rest the meat, still wrapped, for 20 minutes. Unwrap and slice it against the grain, by hand, about 5 mm thick.

  9. Pile the meat onto slices of rye spread with mustard and serve with dill pickles.

Tips for Making Montreal Smoked Meat Sandwich

  • Weigh the curing salt and use pink curing salt no. 1, not pink Himalayan salt. It makes a five-day cure safe and turns the meat pink.
  • Slice against the grain. Brisket cut along the grain is stringy however well it is cooked.
  • No smoker? Skip the smoking, add 1 teaspoon of smoked paprika to the crust and cook the wrapped brisket at 150°C for 5 hours. It will be less smoky but as tender.

Common Substitutions & Variations

  • An eighth of a teaspoon of garlic powder per clove is the usual stand-in when you are out of fresh garlic — it will not caramelise the same way, but it carries the flavour.

What Goes With Montreal Smoked Meat Sandwich

  • Dill pickles
  • A crisp coleslaw
  • French fries

Keeping and Reheating

Keep the cooked, unsliced meat wrapped in the fridge up to 5 days. Reheat slices in a covered pan with a splash of water. Freeze in portions for 2 months.
