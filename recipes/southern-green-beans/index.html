Southern Green Beans

Why Southern Green Beans Works

In the American South, green beans are not cooked until they are crisp and bright. They are cooked for the best part of an hour until they turn soft, olive-coloured and silky, and to a Southern cook that is exactly how they should be. The beans simmer with a piece of smoked pork, usually bacon, a ham hock or a piece of salt pork, and slowly give their sweetness to the liquid while they take on its smoky, salty savour. That liquid is prized in its own right, known as pot likker, and it is meant to be mopped up with cornbread. The tradition comes from a cooking style that valued flavour and tenderness over colour, and it has a practical side too, since older, tougher pod beans needed a long cook to become edible. Modern green beans are more tender, but the long, gentle simmer still gives a texture and depth that a quick sauté cannot match. The method is undemanding. Render the bacon, soften an onion in the fat, add the beans and enough stock to come halfway up them, and leave them to simmer with a lid on. A pinch of sugar rounds the flavour and a splash of vinegar at the end brightens it. Serve them with fried chicken, meatloaf or a plate of ham and cornbread.

How to Cook Southern Green Beans

Before you begin

  • You will need: large pot or casserole.
  • This is a side dish, not a meal on its own. The serving suggestions below say what it goes with.

Tap a step to highlight it, or turn on Cook Mode for large type and step-by-step focus.

  1. Cook the bacon in a large, heavy pot over medium heat for 6 to 8 minutes, until the fat has rendered and the edges are crisp.

  2. Add the onion to the pot and cook in the bacon fat for 5 minutes, stirring, until soft. Add the garlic and cook for 1 minute.

  3. Add the green beans, stock, sugar, pepper and salt. Bring to the boil, then reduce the heat to low.

  4. Cover and simmer for 45 minutes, stirring now and then, until the beans are very soft, dark green and full of flavour.

  5. Uncover and simmer for 5 to 10 minutes if there is a lot of liquid, until it has reduced to a savoury broth.

  6. Stir in the vinegar, then taste and add salt if needed. Serve hot with a spoonful of the cooking liquid.

Notes From the Method

Do not add the salt too early or too generously. The bacon and the stock are already salty, and the liquid reduces as the beans cook.

Snap the beans by hand instead of cutting them with a knife. The rough ends absorb more of the flavour.

For a vegetarian version, swap the bacon for 2 tablespoons of olive oil and 1 teaspoon of smoked paprika, and use vegetable stock.

If You're Missing Something

  • An eighth of a teaspoon of garlic powder per clove is the stand-in usually given when you are out of fresh garlic; it will not brown the same way, and it burns easily, so add it with the liquid.

Leftovers

Keeps in the fridge for up to 4 days and tastes even better the next day. Reheat gently in a covered pan. Freezes for up to 3 months.
