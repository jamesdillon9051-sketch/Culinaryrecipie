Pork Medallions in Cream Sauce

Slices of pork fillet seared in butter and finished in a cream, mustard and white wine sauce with shallots.

Pork Medallions in Cream Sauce: The Method

Tap a step to highlight it, or turn on Cook Mode for large type and step-by-step focus.

  1. Press the pork rounds flat and season with the salt and pepper.

  2. Sear in half the butter in a hot pan for 3 minutes a side until golden and cooked through. Move to a plate.

  3. Add the rest of the butter and soften the shallots for 3 minutes. Pour in the wine and boil until halved.

  4. Stir in the mustard and cream and simmer for 3 minutes until slightly thick.

  5. Return the pork and its juices for 1 minute, add the parsley and serve.

A Few Pointers

  • Slice the pork to an even thickness.
  • Do not overcook the fillet.
  • Reduce the wine before adding cream.
  • Serve at once.

Behind the Recipe

Medallions are simply thick slices of pork fillet, and the name is as grand as the dish gets. The French habit of finishing a seared cut in a pan sauce of wine, cream and mustard is what makes it taste like more than it is.

Slice the fillet into rounds about 2 cm thick and press each flat with your palm so they cook evenly. Season and sear in a hot pan with butter for 3 minutes a side. They should be golden and just cooked through, with no pink. Move them to a plate. Do not overcook them. Fillet is lean, and dries out fast.

Soften the shallots in the same pan, add the wine and boil it down by half, then stir in the mustard and cream and simmer for 3 minutes until it coats a spoon.

Return the pork and its juices to the pan for a minute, add the parsley and serve at once.

Good Alongside

Boiled new potatoes · Green beans · Buttered noodles · Dry white wine

Changing the Recipe

  • Double cream won't whip if you swap it, but for the cooking itself, full-fat crème fraîche or a lower-fat single cream works in its place.
  • A dry vermouth keeps for longer than an open bottle of white wine and is the closer swap than a random white left in the fridge; for an alcohol-free version, use the same amount of stock with a squeeze of lemon.
