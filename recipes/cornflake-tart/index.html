Cornflake Tart

A crisp shortcrust case spread with raspberry jam and filled with cornflakes bound in butter and golden syrup, baked until golden and crunchy. The school dinner classic.

1 hr 30 min prep and cooking plus 45 min chilling and cooling 2 hr 15 min start to finish Serves 8 servings Medium

Make Ahead and Storage

Keeps in an airtight tin for up to 4 days. It is best eaten at room temperature, and can be warmed in a low oven for 10 minutes.

How to Make Cornflake Tart

Before you begin

  • Plan for 45 min of chilling and cooling. It is part of the 2 hr 15 min start to finish.
  • You will need: oven, saucepan, cake or loaf tin, rolling pin (23 cm loose-bottomed tart tin).

Tap a step to highlight it, or turn on Cook Mode for large type and step-by-step focus.

  1. Rub the cold butter into the flour and sugar with your fingertips until the mixture looks like fine breadcrumbs. Stir in the egg yolk and enough water to bring the dough together, then shape into a flat disc, wrap and chill for 30 minutes.

  2. Heat the oven to 190°C / 375°F (170°C / 340°F fan). Roll out the pastry on a floured surface to about 3 mm thick and use it to line a 23 cm loose-bottomed tart tin, pressing it into the corners.

  3. Prick the base with a fork, line with baking paper and fill with baking beans. Bake for 15 minutes, remove the paper and beans and bake for 5 minutes more until pale golden.

  4. Reduce the oven to 180°C / 350°F (160°C / 320°F fan). Spread the jam over the base of the pastry case.

  5. Melt the 60 g of butter with the golden syrup and sugar in a saucepan over low heat, stirring until smooth. Take off the heat and fold in the cornflakes until they are evenly coated.

  6. Spoon the cornflake mixture over the jam and spread it evenly to the edge of the pastry.

  7. Bake for 20 minutes until the topping is golden and crisp. Cool in the tin for 15 minutes before removing and slicing.

Worth Knowing

  • Bake the pastry blind first. The wet jam and syrup would turn an unbaked base soggy, and the crisp case is what you want.
  • Crush the cornflakes lightly, not to dust. Large pieces make the topping crunchy, and a fine crumb makes it heavy.
  • Do not overbake the topping. The syrup goes from golden to burnt quickly, and it hardens as it cools.

Behind the Recipe

The cornflake tart is a British school dinner classic, one of those puddings that people over a certain age can describe in detail: a crisp pastry case, a layer of jam, and a sticky, crunchy top made of cornflakes bound in golden syrup. It is a thrifty dessert made from the store cupboard, and cornflakes were not an obvious choice, but they bake into a brittle, honeyed, caramelised crust that is like a cross between a flapjack and a treacle tart. The tart is made in three stages. The pastry is a simple shortcrust, rubbed together from flour and butter, chilled to relax, rolled and baked blind, so that the base is crisp and does not turn soggy under the jam. Raspberry jam is spread over the base, and the filling is a mixture of melted butter, golden syrup and brown sugar, into which the cornflakes are folded, and which is spread over the jam. The whole thing goes back into the oven, so that the topping crisps and the jam bubbles up around the edges. It is best served warm, with custard, as it always was in the school canteen, or cold in slices with a cup of tea. Crush the cornflakes lightly, not to dust, so that the topping has texture.
