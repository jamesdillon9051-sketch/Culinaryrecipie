Bhindi Fry

Okra pieces cooked with sliced onion, turmeric and amchur in a hot pan until dry, tender and not slimy.

Making Bhindi Fry

Tap a step to highlight it, or turn on Cook Mode for large type and step-by-step focus.

  1. Heat 2 tbsp of the oil in a wide pan. Add the cumin seeds, then the onions, and cook for 6 minutes until golden at the edges. Remove.

  2. Add the remaining oil and the okra to the pan. Cook over a medium-high heat for 12 minutes, stirring only now and then, until the okra is browned and no longer sticky.

  3. Return the onions and add the turmeric, coriander and chilli powder. Cook for 1 minute.

  4. Stir in the amchur and salt and serve at once.

Why It Works

Bhindi is the Hindi word for okra, and this is the dry, pan-cooked way to make it. Okra has a reputation for sliminess, but the sliminess is easy to manage, and there are three rules.

The first is that the okra must be completely dry before it is cut. Wash it, then spread it on a clean cloth for an hour, or wipe each pod. Water on the surface activates the mucilage. The second is to cut it into rounds about 1 cm thick, and leave the cut surface untouched in the pan for the first minutes. High heat dries the surface before the slime forms. The third is not to add salt until the end, since salt draws out liquid.

Cook the onion first, until it browns at the edges, then the okra, over a medium-high heat for 12 minutes with only occasional stirring. Do not cover the pan. Steam makes the okra gluey.

Amchur (dried mango powder) goes in at the end for a sour finish; lemon juice does the same job. Add the salt and a little chilli powder, and serve at once.

Leftovers, Reheating and Keeping

Best eaten the day it is made. Keeps in the fridge for 1 day, though the okra softens.
