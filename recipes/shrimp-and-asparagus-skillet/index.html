Shrimp and Asparagus Skillet

Shrimp and asparagus cooked in one pan with garlic, lemon and a little smoked paprika, with no flour or sugar. Four servings in 20 minutes.

The Idea Behind Shrimp and Asparagus Skillet

Shrimp go from tender to rubbery in about a minute, and that is the usual way this dish goes wrong. The fix is three things: a hot pan, shrimp patted dry, and the heat off as soon as they curl into a loose C. Shrimp curled into a tight O are overcooked.

Asparagus goes first. It needs a few minutes longer than the shrimp, so cook it until bright green, then set it aside and let it wait while the shrimp take their turn. The shrimp need about 2 minutes a side in a single layer, so work in two batches if your pan is small. A crowded pan steams them instead of searing.

Garlic goes in last, for 30 seconds only, because it turns bitter if it browns. Lemon juice and zest finish the pan and loosen the browned bits into a light sauce. Serve straight away; shrimp do not reheat well.

How to Make Shrimp and Asparagus Skillet

Tap a step to highlight it, or turn on Cook Mode for large type and step-by-step focus.

  1. Pat the shrimp dry and toss them with the paprika, half the salt and the pepper.

  2. Heat 1 tbsp of the oil in a large frying pan over medium-high heat. Add the asparagus and cook for 4 minutes, stirring now and then, until bright green and tender-crisp. Move to a plate.

  3. Add the remaining oil, then the shrimp in a single layer. Cook for 2 minutes without moving them, turn and cook for 1 to 2 minutes more, until pink and curled into a loose C.

  4. Add the garlic and cook for 30 seconds. Return the asparagus to the pan with the lemon juice, lemon zest and the remaining salt, toss for 30 seconds and scatter with the parsley.

Small Things That Help

  • Buy shrimp that are already peeled and deveined to save time at the board.
  • Defrost frozen shrimp overnight in the fridge, or in a bowl of cold water, and pat them dry before they hit the pan.
  • If the pan looks crowded, cook the shrimp in two batches so they sear instead of steam.

Common Substitutions & Variations

  • An eighth of a teaspoon of garlic powder per clove is the usual stand-in when you are out of fresh garlic — it will not caramelise the same way, but it carries the flavour.

What Goes With Shrimp and Asparagus Skillet

  • Cauliflower rice
  • A green salad
  • Zucchini noodles

Keeping and Reheating

Best eaten straight away. Keeps in the fridge for up to 2 days; eat it cold in a salad, since shrimp turn rubbery when reheated.
