Wonton Soup

Silky wontons with a prawn and pork filling floating in a clear, restorative broth. The wrappers are so thin they turn translucent, and the whole thing comes together in half an hour.

Why This Recipe Works

Wontons are cooked in a separate pan of water rather than in the soup, because the starch they shed would cloud a broth that should be crystal clear. Whole prawns chopped by hand rather than minced keep bouncy pieces in the filling, giving texture against the soft pork.

Method

Tap a step to highlight it, or turn on Cook Mode for large type and step-by-step focus.

  1. Mix the pork with the soy sauce, Shaoxing wine, ginger, white pepper, salt and cornflour, stirring in one direction for 2 minutes until sticky.

  2. Fold in the chopped prawns, spring onions and sesame oil. Chill 20 minutes.

  3. Simmer the stock with the sliced ginger and whole spring onions for 15 minutes, then strain and season with soy sauce and sesame oil. Keep hot.

  4. To wrap, put a teaspoon of filling in the centre of a wrapper, wet the edges, and fold into a triangle, pressing out all the air.

  5. Bring the two long corners together around your finger and pinch to seal, so it sits like a little bundle.

  6. Bring a separate large pan of water to a gentle boil and cook the wontons in two batches for 3 to 4 minutes, until the wrappers turn translucent and they float.

  7. Blanch the pak choi in the same water for 60 seconds.

  8. Divide the wontons and greens between warmed bowls and ladle the hot broth over.

  9. Scatter with spring onion and serve immediately.

Chef’s Tips

  • Press all the air out as you seal, or the wontons will burst in the water.
  • A good stock is the whole soup here. Use homemade if you possibly can.
  • Wontons freeze superbly — cook from frozen, adding 2 minutes.

Pairing Suggestions

  • Jasmine tea or a light lager
  • Serve as a starter, or with noodles added for a full meal
  • Follow with orange segments

Storage & Reheating

Uncooked wontons freeze 2 months. Store leftover broth and wontons separately; wontons left in liquid turn to paste.
