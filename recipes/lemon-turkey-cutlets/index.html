Lemon Turkey Cutlets

Thin turkey cutlets lightly floured and pan-fried, then turned through a quick sauce of lemon, unsalted stock, butter and parsley. Four servings in 22 minutes.

Why This Lemon Turkey Cutlets Recipe Works

Thin cutlets, hot pan. Turkey breast has even less fat than chicken, so it dries out the moment it is overcooked, and 2 minutes a side is all a thin cutlet needs. Everything else is there to keep it moist and give it flavour.

A thin coat of flour and egg white shields the meat from the pan, and it browns into a light crust. Shake off the surplus flour, or it burns in the oil and turns the sauce gluey. Cook two cutlets at a time. Crowding the pan drops the heat and the coating turns pale and soft.

The sauce is made in the pan: lemon juice, unsalted stock, a little butter and parsley, simmered for 2 minutes. Lemon zest goes in at the end, where it keeps its fragrance. There are no capers and no salt, which is why the lemon is generous. Serve at once. Rice or green beans catch the sauce.

Making Lemon Turkey Cutlets

Tap a step to highlight it, or turn on Cook Mode for large type and step-by-step focus.

  1. Spread the flour on a plate and beat the egg white in a shallow dish. Season the turkey with the pepper.

  2. Press each cutlet into the flour, tap off the excess, then turn it in the egg white.

  3. Warm half the oil in a large frying pan over medium-high heat. Pan-fry two cutlets for 2 minutes a side, until golden, and set aside. Repeat with the remaining oil and cutlets.

  4. Pour the lemon juice and stock into the pan, add the butter and simmer for 2 minutes. Add the zest, return the cutlets, turn to coat and scatter with the parsley.

Notes for Getting It Right

  • If your cutlets are thick, slice them in half horizontally; thin cutlets cook in the time given.
  • Keep the first batch warm on a plate but uncovered, so that steam does not soften the crust.
  • Add the zest off the heat so that it stays fragrant.

Swaps and Variations

  • Vegetable stock in place of the chicken stock makes this meat-free without changing the method, if that is what you need.

Adapting It for Your Diet

  • A measure-for-measure gluten-free flour blend can generally replace the plain flour here without changing the method, since this is savoury cooking rather than a structural bake.

Serving Suggestions

  • Steamed white rice
  • Steamed green beans
  • A cucumber salad

Leftovers and Storage

Best eaten at once. Keeps in the fridge for up to 3 days; reheat in a hot oven to crisp the crust.
