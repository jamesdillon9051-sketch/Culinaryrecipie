Tostones

Green plantains fried, smashed flat and fried again until crisp and golden, and salted. Served with a garlic and lime mojo for dipping.

The Case for Tostones

Tostones are green plantains that are fried twice: the first time to cook them through and soften them, and the second, after being smashed flat, to crisp them into golden, salted discs. They are a staple of Puerto Rico, the Dominican Republic, Cuba and much of the Caribbean and Latin America, where they are served as a snack, an appetiser or a side dish with rice and beans, and they are eaten hot, since a cold tostone goes hard. The plantains must be green and firm, so they are starchy and not sweet, and the first fry is gentle. The garlic mojo is the dip: raw garlic, lime and olive oil, and it is what makes them addictive.

What Goes With It

  • Garlic mojo
  • Roast pork
  • A cold beer

How to Make Tostones

Tap a step to highlight it, or turn on Cook Mode for large type and step-by-step focus.

  1. Stir the garlic, olive oil, lime juice and salt together for the mojo and set aside.

  2. Cut the ends off the plantains, slit the skin lengthways and peel. Cut into rounds 2.5 cm thick.

  3. Heat 2 cm of oil in a heavy pan to 160°C / 320°F. Fry the plantain rounds in batches for 3 minutes per side, until pale golden and tender. Drain on paper towels.

  4. While they are still warm, smash each round to about 1 cm thick with the bottom of a glass or a small plate.

  5. Raise the oil temperature to 180°C / 350°F and fry the smashed plantains for 2 to 3 minutes per side, until deep golden and crisp.

  6. Drain, sprinkle with the salt and serve hot with the mojo.

Notes From the Method

Use green plantains. Yellow ones with black spots make sweet, soft maduros instead.

Smash them while they are still warm. Cold plantain cracks.

Fry twice, at two temperatures. The first fry cooks them and the second crisps them.

Ingredient Swaps

  • Lemon is the everyday swap for lime in this kind of dish — the acidity plays the same role even though the flavour is not identical.
  • An eighth of a teaspoon of garlic powder per clove is the stand-in usually given when you are out of fresh garlic; it will not brown the same way, and it burns easily, so add it with the liquid.

Leftovers

Best eaten at once. Re-crisp leftovers in a hot oven for 8 minutes.
