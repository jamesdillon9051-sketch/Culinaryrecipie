Chewy brown sugar cookies loaded with oats, chocolate chips, pretzels, coconut and pecans, baked for 12 minutes.

The Case for Kitchen Sink Cookies

The name comes from the idea of putting in everything but the kitchen sink. A kitchen sink cookie is a chewy base dough crowded with whatever is in the cupboard, and it is a good way to use up the remnants of half-finished packets of chocolate, nuts and crisps.

The key is balance. Too many add-ins and the dough will not hold together; too few and it is just a chocolate chip cookie. For one batch, use about 300 g of mix-ins to 250 g of flour. Choose a mix of textures: crunchy pretzels, chewy coconut, rich pecans, creamy chocolate and hearty oats. Salty pretzels against sweet chocolate and brown sugar is the combination people come back for.

Chop the larger pieces. Break pretzels into small shards and chop the pecans roughly, so that no piece is bigger than a coin. Large pieces create weak points where the cookie breaks, and they push the dough apart in the oven.

Chill the dough for 30 minutes before baking, since the weight of the add-ins means it spreads quickly in warm conditions. Bake until the edges are golden brown and the centre looks slightly underdone, and let the cookies rest on the tray for 10 minutes.

Making Kitchen Sink Cookies, Step by Step

Before you begin

  • Plan for 30 min of chilling. It is part of the 57 min start to finish.
  • You will need: oven.

Tap a step to highlight it, or turn on Cook Mode for large type and step-by-step focus.

  1. Beat the butter and both sugars for 2 minutes until fluffy. Beat in the egg and vanilla.

  2. Mix in the flour, oats, bicarbonate of soda and salt.

  3. Fold in the chocolate chips, pretzels, coconut and pecans. Chill the dough for 30 minutes.

  4. Heat the oven to 180°C / 355°F and line 2 trays. Roll into 24 balls and place 5 cm apart.

  5. Bake for 12 minutes until the edges are golden brown. Cool on the trays for 10 minutes.
