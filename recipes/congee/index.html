Century Egg Congee

Rice cooked in eight times its volume of stock until the grains collapse into a silken porridge, served with century egg and pork. Breakfast across southern China, and the first thing anyone eats when recovering from illness.

Why This Recipe Works

Freezing the rinsed rice before cooking cracks the grain structure, so it breaks down in about half the usual time. Stirring in one direction periodically shears the starch out of the grains, which is what turns the liquid from thin rice soup into a creamy suspension.

Method

Tap a step to highlight it, or turn on Cook Mode for large type and step-by-step focus.

  1. Rinse the rice until the water runs clear, drain thoroughly, toss with the oil and a pinch of salt, and freeze for at least 2 hours.

  2. Marinate the pork with the soy sauce, cornflour and sesame oil for 20 minutes.

  3. Bring the stock and ginger to the boil in a large heavy pot. Add the frozen rice directly, without thawing.

  4. Return to the boil, then reduce to a gentle simmer and cook uncovered for 45 to 60 minutes, stirring in one direction every 10 minutes and scraping the base.

  5. The congee is ready when the grains have burst and the texture is thick, smooth and pourable. Add hot water if it tightens too far.

  6. Stir in the salt, then slide in the pork slices one by one and simmer 4 minutes until just cooked.

  7. Add half the century egg and stir it through so it dissolves slightly and flavours the whole pot.

  8. Ladle into bowls and top with the remaining century egg, spring onions, fried shallots and a heavy grind of white pepper, with soy and chilli oil at the table.

Chef’s Tips

  • The freezing trick genuinely halves the cooking time and is the single most useful thing about this recipe.
  • Stir in one direction only. Changing direction breaks the starch suspension and the congee can turn watery.
  • If century eggs are not to your taste, poached chicken, salted fish or plain ginger and spring onion are all traditional.

Pairing Suggestions

  • Youtiao for dipping
  • Pickled mustard greens
  • Hot Chinese tea

Storage & Reheating

Refrigerate up to 4 days. It sets solid when cold — reheat with plenty of extra stock or water, whisking to loosen.
