Arborio rice, stock, onion, white wine and parmesan cooked on high for 2 hours 30 minutes in the slow cooker into a creamy risotto.

Slow Cooker Risotto: Step by Step

Tap a step to highlight it, or turn on Cook Mode for large type and step-by-step focus.

  1. Melt the 40 g butter in a pan and cook the onion for 5 minutes. Add the garlic and rice and stir for 2 minutes until the grains turn translucent at the edges.

  2. Pour in the wine and let it sizzle away. Tip the rice into the slow cooker.

  3. Add the hot stock, salt and pepper and stir once.

  4. Cover and cook on high for 2 hours 30 minutes, until the rice is tender with a slight bite.

  5. Stir in the parmesan and cubed butter vigorously for 1 minute, scatter with parsley and serve.

Keep an Eye on These

  1. Use arborio rice for a creamy result.
  2. Do not lift the lid before 2 hours.
  3. Add a splash of hot stock if it sets.

Common Substitutions & Variations

  • An eighth of a teaspoon of garlic powder per clove is the stand-in usually given when you are out of fresh garlic; it will not brown the same way, and it burns easily, so add it with the liquid.
  • Vegetable stock in place of the chicken stock makes this meat-free without changing the method, if that is what you need.
  • A dry vermouth keeps for longer than an open bottle of white wine and is the closer swap than a random white left in the fridge; for an alcohol-free version, use the same amount of stock with a squeeze of lemon.

Good to Know About Slow Cooker Risotto

Most people think a risotto needs 20 minutes of stirring. It does not, if you are willing to change the method. In a slow cooker the rice cooks gently in a closed pot, the starch releases slowly, and the result is creamy and evenly cooked without anyone standing over it.

The key is to use arborio rice, which has the high starch that makes a risotto creamy, and to toast it in butter on the hob first. A couple of minutes in the pan with the onion and garlic coats each grain in fat, which keeps them separate and nutty. Then the wine goes in, sizzles away, and everything is transferred to the cooker with hot stock.

Do not lift the lid or add more rice than the recipe says. A slow cooker has little evaporation, so the ratio of liquid to rice is more exact than on the hob. Use 4 parts stock to 1 part rice by volume, and stir only at the end. Check at 2 hours.

Finish with the butter and parmesan off the heat, stirring vigorously for a minute, which brings the creamy texture together. A final splash of hot stock loosens it if it has set. Serve at once in warm bowls, as risotto stiffens as it cools.

On the Table Alongside

Roast chicken · Green salad · Grilled mushrooms · White wine

Storing Leftovers

Best eaten at once. Leftover risotto makes good rice cakes.
