Frikadeller

Danish pork and veal meatballs, flattened into ovals and fried in butter until brown on both sides, served with potatoes and pickled cucumber.

Why This Recipe Works

The mince is beaten rather than folded, for several minutes, until it becomes sticky and pale — that developed protein is what holds the meatball together without egg doing all the work, and what gives frikadeller their springy bite. Resting the mixture in the fridge lets the breadcrumbs finish absorbing the milk.

Method

Tap a step to highlight it, or turn on Cook Mode for large type and step-by-step focus.

  1. Make the pickle first: warm the vinegar, sugar and salt until dissolved, cool, and pour over the cucumber. Leave at least 1 hour.

  2. Soak the breadcrumbs in the milk for 10 minutes.

  3. Put both minces, the grated onion, soaked breadcrumbs, egg, salt, pepper and allspice in a bowl.

  4. Beat with a wooden spoon or the paddle of a mixer for 4 to 5 minutes, until the mixture turns noticeably paler, stickier and smoother. This is the step that makes frikadeller.

  5. Cover and refrigerate 30 minutes.

  6. Melt half the butter in a large frying pan over medium heat.

  7. Shape the mixture into ovals using two wet tablespoons, dropping them straight into the pan and flattening slightly.

  8. Fry 5 to 6 minutes a side until deep brown and cooked through, adding the rest of the butter for the second batch.

  9. Serve with boiled potatoes, the drained pickled cucumber and plenty of parsley.

Chef’s Tips

  • Beat the mixture until it changes colour. Gentle mixing gives crumbly meatballs.
  • Two wet spoons make the traditional oval shape and stop the mince sticking.
  • Medium heat and real butter. Rushed frikadeller are dark outside and pink inside.

Pairing Suggestions

  • Boiled potatoes and brown gravy
  • Pickled beetroot
  • A cold lager

Storage & Reheating

Refrigerate up to 3 days and eat cold or reheated. Freezes 3 months, cooked.
