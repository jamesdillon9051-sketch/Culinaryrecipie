The Case for Energy Balls

Energy balls, also called energy bites or bliss balls, are the snack of the healthy-eating world of the past two decades, and they earned their place by being practical. They are made from ingredients that are calorie-dense and slow to digest, dates, oats, nuts or nut butter and seeds, blended and rolled into pieces that fit in the hand, and they need no baking. Dates are the base and the binder. Their sticky sweetness holds everything together and does away with the need for added sugar, while peanut butter and oats give protein, fibre and staying power, chia seeds add omega-3 fats, and a little honey and chocolate turn it from health food into a treat. They are useful as a snack before or after exercise, in a lunchbox, or on a walk, and the mixture is easy to vary: almond butter for peanut, cocoa for extra chocolate, dried cranberries for chocolate chips. The one thing to look for is the texture of the mixture: it should stay together when squeezed, and if it is dry, a spoonful of water or milk brings it back.

How to Make Energy Balls

Tap a step to highlight it, or turn on Cook Mode for large type and step-by-step focus.

  1. If the dates are firm, cover them with warm water for 10 minutes, then drain them well.

  2. Blend the dates, peanut butter, honey, vanilla and salt in a food processor to a thick, sticky paste.

  3. Add the oats and chia seeds and pulse until the mixture is crumbly but holds together when you squeeze a little in your hand.

  4. Stir in the chocolate chips by hand.

  5. With damp hands, roll the mixture into 24 balls of about 18 g each, then roll them in the coconut.

  6. Refrigerate the balls for 30 minutes to firm up.

A Few Notes

Use soft, fresh Medjool dates. Dry ones make a crumbly mix, and soaking them briefly puts it right.

Check the texture by squeezing. If the mixture will not hold, add a teaspoon of water at a time, and if it is sticky, add a spoonful of oats.

Wet your hands before rolling. The mixture is sticky, and damp hands keep it from clinging.

Keeping What's Left

Keep in a lidded container in the fridge for up to 10 days, or freeze for up to 3 months and thaw for 15 minutes at room temperature before eating.
