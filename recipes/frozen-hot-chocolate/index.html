Frozen Hot Chocolate

Ice, milk, cocoa and chocolate sauce blended into a thick frozen drink, topped with whipped cream and shaved chocolate.

Frozen Hot Chocolate: Step by Step

Tap a step to highlight it, or turn on Cook Mode for large type and step-by-step focus.

  1. Whisk the cocoa with 3 tbsp of the fresh milk to make a smooth paste, then stir in the rest of the fresh milk.

  2. Pour into a blender with the chocolate syrup, vanilla and salt.

  3. Add the frozen milk cubes and blend in short bursts, scraping down the sides, until thick and smooth.

  4. Pour into 2 tall glasses.

  5. Top with the whipped cream and chocolate shavings and serve with wide straws.

Things to Watch For

  1. Freeze milk into cubes the night before.
  2. Make the cocoa paste first.
  3. Serve straight away.

About This Recipe

The idea is a New York invention of the late twentieth century, a drink which has the flavour of hot chocolate and the texture of a slushy. It was created in a restaurant to serve in summer, and it caught on with people who wanted cocoa in July. The result is a thick, cold, frosty glass that is part milkshake, part ice cream.

The base is cocoa and chocolate sauce, which together give a deep flavour. Use unsweetened cocoa powder, sifted so that it does not clump, and a good quality chocolate syrup. Whisk the cocoa with a little warm milk first to dissolve it into a smooth paste, then cool it down in the fridge, because a lump of dry cocoa will not blend into cold milk.

Use whole milk ice cubes. Freeze a tray of milk the night before, and blend those instead of ordinary ice. They keep the drink creamy as they melt, where water ice dilutes it. The result is thick, icy and intensely chocolatey.

Blend in short bursts, scraping down the sides, until the texture is like a thick smoothie. Pour into tall glasses, top with whipped cream and chocolate shavings and serve straight away with a wide straw. It melts fast, so do not leave it standing.

Brownies · Cookies · Summer treat · Whipped cream
