Empanadas de Pino

Large baked empanadas filled with beef, plenty of onion, cumin, raisins, olive and hard-boiled egg. The national empanada of Chile.

Before You Start

Cook the onions until they are sweet and soft. Undercooked onion makes a sharp filling.

Cool the filling before you fill. Warm pino melts the dough and makes it hard to seal.

Do not overfill. The pastry splits in the oven if the filling is too full.

Making Empanadas de Pino

Tap a step to highlight it, or turn on Cook Mode for large type and step-by-step focus.

  1. Mix the flour and salt, then stir in the melted butter, egg yolk and milk to a soft dough. Knead for 5 minutes, wrap and rest for 30 minutes.

  2. Heat the oil in a large pan over medium heat and cook the onions for 15 minutes, until very soft. Add the beef, cumin, paprika, oregano, salt and pepper and cook for 10 minutes, breaking up the meat, until browned.

  3. Stir in the tablespoon of flour and cook for 1 minute. Spread the pino on a tray and cool completely, at least 1 hour.

  4. Heat the oven to 200°C / 400°F and line two baking trays with baking paper.

  5. Roll the dough to 3 mm thick and cut 8 rounds of about 18 cm across, re-rolling the trimmings.

  6. Put 4 tablespoons of pino on each round, with a slice of egg, 2 olives and a few raisins. Fold the dough over, press the edges together and crimp with a fork.

  7. Set the empanadas on the trays, brush with the beaten egg and bake for 25 to 30 minutes, until golden brown.

What's Going On Here

The empanada de pino is the national empanada of Chile, a large half-moon pastry filled with a mixture called pino, which is beef and a great deal of onion, cooked slowly with cumin and paprika, and then studded with a slice of hard-boiled egg, an olive and a few raisins. The onions are the secret. There is as much onion as meat, and they are cooked until they are soft and sweet, which makes the filling moist and rich. The sweet raisin and salty olive are what mark it as Chilean. The pastry is soft and buttery, more like a bread than a shortcrust, and it is baked, not fried, until it is golden. The filling must be cool before it goes in the dough.

After You've Cooked It

Keep in the fridge up to 3 days and reheat in a 180°C oven for 12 minutes. Freeze baked empanadas for 2 months.

Swaps and Variations

  • Fresh herbs work in place of the dried oregano at roughly three times the quantity — a teaspoon of dried is about a tablespoon of fresh, chopped.

If You Need to Adjust It

  • A measure-for-measure gluten-free flour blend can generally replace the plain flour here without changing the method, since this is savoury cooking rather than a structural bake.
