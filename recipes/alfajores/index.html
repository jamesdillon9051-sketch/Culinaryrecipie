Alfajores

Two cornflour-heavy shortbread biscuits sandwiching dulce de leche and rolled in desiccated coconut. Argentinian and Peruvian in equal measure, and they dissolve on the tongue rather than crumbling.

Why This Recipe Works

Roughly half the flour is replaced with cornflour, which has no gluten at all, so the biscuits have almost no structure and simply dissolve. That also makes the dough fragile, which is why it must be chilled hard before cutting and handled as little as possible.

Method

Tap a step to highlight it, or turn on Cook Mode for large type and step-by-step focus.

  1. Beat the butter and sugar for 4 minutes until pale and fluffy.

  2. Beat in the egg yolks one at a time, then the brandy, lemon zest and vanilla.

  3. Sift together the cornflour, plain flour, baking powder, bicarbonate and salt.

  4. Add the dry ingredients and mix on the lowest speed only until the dough just comes together. It will be very soft.

  5. Wrap and refrigerate at least 1 hour, until firm.

  6. Roll out between two sheets of baking paper to 6 mm thick — never on a floured surface, as this dough tears.

  7. Cut 5 cm rounds and lift them with a palette knife onto lined trays. Chill the cut biscuits 20 minutes.

  8. Bake at 175°C / 345°F for 10 to 12 minutes until set but still pale — they should not brown at all.

  9. Cool completely on the trays, as they are extremely fragile while warm.

  10. Pipe or spoon a generous mound of dulce de leche onto half the biscuits, sandwich gently with the rest, and roll the exposed filling edge in coconut. Dust the tops with icing sugar.

Chef’s Tips

  • Pale is correct. A browned alfajor has been overbaked and loses the melting texture.
  • Roll between paper, not on flour. Extra flour toughens an already delicate dough.
  • Use a thick dulce de leche (repostero grade) or the biscuits will slide apart.

Pairing Suggestions

  • Strong coffee or maté
  • A glass of cold milk
  • Nothing — they are very sweet

Storage & Reheating

Keeps 5 days in an airtight tin and improves after a day as the biscuits soften into the filling.
