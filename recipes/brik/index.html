Tunisian Brik

A sheet of malsouka pastry folded around tuna, capers, parsley and a whole raw egg, then deep fried so the yolk is still liquid when you bite in.

Why This Recipe Works

The whole point is the runny yolk, which means the brik goes into hot oil the moment the egg is in and comes out in ninety seconds — long enough for the pastry to crisp, not long enough for the yolk to set. Making a small well of filling for the egg to sit in stops it sliding to the edge and leaking during the fold.

Method

Tap a step to highlight it, or turn on Cook Mode for large type and step-by-step focus.

  1. Mix the tuna, onion, capers, parsley, mashed potato if using, harissa, caraway, salt and pepper.

  2. Heat the oil to 180C in a wide, shallow pan. Have a slotted spoon ready before you start.

  3. Lay one pastry sheet on a plate. Spoon a quarter of the filling onto one half, and shape it into a ring with a well in the middle.

  4. Crack an egg into the well. Work quickly from here.

  5. Fold the pastry over into a half moon or triangle and press the edges together firmly.

  6. Slide it straight into the hot oil, using the plate to help it in.

  7. Fry 45 seconds, spooning hot oil over the top, then turn carefully and fry 40 seconds more.

  8. Lift out when golden and crisp and drain briefly on a rack.

  9. Serve immediately with lemon, and eat it by biting a corner and drinking the yolk.

Chef’s Tips

  • Ninety seconds total. Any longer and the yolk sets and the point is lost.
  • Build a well of filling for the egg to sit in.
  • Have the oil at temperature before you crack the egg, not after.

Pairing Suggestions

  • Lemon wedges and harissa
  • A Tunisian salad
  • Mint tea

Storage & Reheating

Eat immediately. The filling keeps 2 days refrigerated.
