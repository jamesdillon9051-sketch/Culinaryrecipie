Chakalaka

A South African relish of onions, peppers, carrot and beans cooked hard with curry powder and chilli — served with everything from pap to a braai, hot or cold.

Why This Recipe Works

The carrot is grated rather than diced, which is what gives chakalaka its shredded texture and lets it cook in minutes instead of twenty. The curry powder is fried in the oil before any wet ingredient goes near it — thrown into the vegetables later it tastes raw and dusty, and that one step is the difference between a good chakalaka and a dull one.

Method

Tap a step to highlight it, or turn on Cook Mode for large type and step-by-step focus.

  1. Heat the oil in a wide pan and cook the onions over medium-high heat for 5 minutes until softening and just colouring.

  2. Add the peppers and cook 3 minutes.

  3. Add the garlic, ginger, chillies, curry powder and paprika and fry, stirring, for 90 seconds. It should smell toasted, not raw.

  4. Add the grated carrot and cook 3 minutes, stirring.

  5. Add the tomatoes and salt and simmer 5 minutes until thickened.

  6. Stir in the baked beans and warm through for 2 minutes only — cooked longer they collapse.

  7. Take off the heat, stir in the vinegar and coriander, and taste for salt and heat.

Chef’s Tips

  • Fry the curry powder in the oil. Everything else here is easy; this is the step that carries the dish.
  • Grate the carrot. Diced, it stays hard and the texture is wrong.
  • It is as good cold as hot, and better the next day either way.

Pairing Suggestions

  • Boerewors from the braai
  • Pap or samp
  • A cold Castle lager

Storage & Reheating

Refrigerate up to 5 days and freeze 3 months. Traditionally eaten cold from the fridge as often as hot.
