What Makes Ground Beef and Cabbage Skillet Work

Cabbage, onion, mince and garlic: four cheap things and about thirty minutes. The cabbage is added in handfuls so it wilts as you go, and a whole head that looked impossible to fit disappears into a single pan.

The mince is browned first. It gives off enough fat to cook the cabbage in, so little added oil is needed.

The cabbage should still have some bite when you take it off the heat, soft at the edges and a little crisp at the stem. Overcooked cabbage smells sulphurous.

Finish with a splash of vinegar or soy sauce. Cabbage and beef are mild together, and a little acid wakes them both up. Because everything cooks in one pan, the cabbage drinks up the beef fat and tastes richer than it looks. A spoonful of rice or a baked potato on the side turns it into a full meal for very little extra effort.

Making Ground Beef and Cabbage Skillet

Tap a step to highlight it, or turn on Cook Mode for large type and step-by-step focus.

  1. Heat a large deep frying pan over medium-high heat. Add the mince and cook for 8 minutes, breaking it up, until browned.

  2. Add the onion and garlic and cook for 3 minutes.

  3. Add the cabbage in two handfuls, stirring until the first has wilted before adding the second. Cook for 6 minutes in total.

  4. Stir in the soy sauce, vinegar, pepper and salt. Cook for 1 minute and serve straight away.

Notes for Getting It Right

  • Add the cabbage in batches so the pan stays hot.
  • If the pan looks dry, add a splash of water rather than more oil.
  • Slice the cabbage thinly; thick pieces take longer to soften.

Changing the Recipe

  • An eighth of a teaspoon of garlic powder per clove is the usual stand-in when you are out of fresh garlic — it will not caramelise the same way, but it carries the flavour.
  • A dry vermouth keeps for longer than an open bottle of white wine and is the closer swap than a random white left in the fridge; for an alcohol-free version, use the same amount of stock with a squeeze of lemon.

Dietary Notes

  • Tamari is the direct swap for soy sauce if you need the dish gluten-free — it is brewed the same way, just without the wheat.

What to Serve with Ground Beef and Cabbage Skillet

  • Steamed rice
  • Fried egg
  • Chilli sauce
  • Buttered noodles

Keeping and Reheating

Keeps in the fridge for up to 3 days. Reheat in a pan or the microwave. The cabbage softens on reheating, so eat it fresh if you prefer a bite.
