Kassler

Smoked cured pork loin roasted with sauerkraut and onions for 90 minutes until tender, a German family dinner.

It looks like a ham and eats like a roast pork, which is the difference smoking and curing make. Kassler is a smoked, cured pork loin, pink inside and mild, and it only needs gentle heat to warm it through and make it tender.

Soften onions in lard in a casserole, add the sauerkraut, a grated apple, juniper and caraway and pour in white wine and stock. Set the kassler on top. Cover the casserole and keep the oven low, at 160C, because the joint is already cured and cooks to a firm, dry meat if it is rushed. The sauerkraut gives off steam that keeps it moist.

After 90 minutes the meat should be tender when pierced and the cabbage soft. Lift it out, rest it for 10 minutes and carve in thick slices. Serve on the sauerkraut with mashed potato or boiled potatoes and mustard. The cabbage is better the next day, and leftover slices fry well.

Questions About Kassler

How long does Kassler take to make?

About 1 hour 45 minutes in total — 15 minutes of preparation and 1 hour 30 minutes of cooking. The recipe serves 4.

What are the key tips for making Kassler?

Keep the oven low, as the meat is already cured. Cover the casserole to keep the cabbage moist. Slice thickly so the meat stays juicy.

What should I serve with Kassler?

Mashed potato; Boiled potatoes; Mustard; Riesling.

Can Kassler be made ahead or frozen?

Keeps for 4 days in the fridge. Freeze for 2 months.

Is Kassler suitable for a special diet?

This recipe is dairy-free, high-protein and no added sugar. Those labels are checked against the ingredient list, so a dish is only marked when nothing in it contradicts the claim.

How many calories are in Kassler?

About 613 kcal per serving, with 66 g protein, 22 g carbohydrate and 29 g fat. The figures are calculated from the ingredient list and are an estimate.

The Method for Kassler

Tap a step to highlight it, or turn on Cook Mode for large type and step-by-step focus.

  1. Heat the oven to 160C. Soften the onions in the lard in a casserole for 5 minutes.

  2. Add the sauerkraut, apple, juniper, caraway, wine and stock and stir.

  3. Set the kassler on top, cover tightly and cook for 90 minutes.

  4. Rest the meat for 10 minutes and carve in thick slices. Serve on the sauerkraut.

Worth Getting Right

  • Keep the oven low, as the meat is already cured.
  • Cover the casserole to keep the cabbage moist.
  • Slice thickly so the meat stays juicy.

Ingredient Swaps

  • Vegetable stock in place of the chicken stock makes this meat-free without changing the method, if that is what you need.
  • A dry vermouth keeps for longer than an open bottle of white wine and is the closer swap than a random white left in the fridge; for an alcohol-free version, use the same amount of stock with a squeeze of lemon.

Pairs Well With

Mashed potato · Boiled potatoes · Mustard · Riesling

Storage

Keeps for 4 days in the fridge. Freeze for 2 months.
