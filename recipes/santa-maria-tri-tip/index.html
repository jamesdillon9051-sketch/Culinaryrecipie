The Case for Santa Maria Tri-Tip

Tri-tip is the barbecue cut of California's Central Coast. It is a triangular piece of beef from the bottom of the sirloin, and for most of the twentieth century it was ground up or sold cheaply, until the butchers and cooks of Santa Maria began to grill it over coals of local red oak. The Santa Maria style is famously simple: a rub of salt, black pepper and garlic powder, nothing more, then fire. The roast is seared over direct heat to build a dark crust and moved to a cooler part of the grill to finish gently, so the middle turns rosy and juicy rather than grey. It is served the traditional way, in thin slices with pinquito beans, fresh tomato salsa and a grilled bread roll. Because tri-tip is lean, it needs watching. Take it off the heat at 52°C / 125°F for medium-rare, since it rises a few degrees as it rests, and it goes dry and chewy beyond medium. The other trick is in the carving. The roast has two muscles with the grain running in different directions, so find the point where the grain changes, cut the roast in two there, and slice each half across the grain. Done right, it eats like a much more expensive steak.

How to Make Santa Maria Tri-Tip

Tap a step to highlight it, or turn on Cook Mode for large type and step-by-step focus.

  1. Rub the tri-tip with the oil. Mix the salt, pepper, garlic powder and paprika and press it all over the roast. Leave it at room temperature for 30 minutes.

  2. Light a charcoal grill and bank the coals to one side, or set a gas grill to high on one side only. You want a hot zone at about 230°C / 450°F and a cooler zone with no heat under it.

  3. Grill the roast over the hot side for 4 to 5 minutes on each side, until a dark crust forms. Watch for flare-ups from the fat.

  4. Move the roast to the cooler side, close the lid and grill for 15 to 25 minutes, until a thermometer pushed into the thickest part reads 52°C / 125°F for medium-rare or 57°C / 135°F for medium.

  5. Transfer to a board, tent loosely with foil and rest for 10 minutes. The temperature will rise by 3 to 5 degrees.

  6. Find the point where the grain of the meat changes direction and cut the roast in two there. Slice each half thinly across the grain and serve with the salsa.

Notes From the Method

Use a thermometer. Tri-tip is lean and the difference between pink and dry is only about 10 degrees, so time alone is not a reliable guide.

In the oven, sear the roast in a heavy pan for 3 minutes per side, then roast at 220°C / 425°F (200°C fan) for 18 to 25 minutes to the same temperatures.

Slice against the grain and keep the slices thin. Cut with the grain and even a perfectly cooked tri-tip turns chewy.

Storing and Reheating

Leftover slices keep in the fridge for up to 4 days and make excellent sandwiches, served cold or warmed briefly in a hot pan. Freeze for up to 2 months.
