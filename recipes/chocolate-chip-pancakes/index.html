Chocolate Chip Pancakes

Fluffy buttermilk pancakes studded with chocolate chips, cooked 2 minutes a side on a medium griddle.

Keep the heat low. That is most of the recipe, and the part people skip. Chocolate chips scorch quickly, and a pan that is hot enough to brown a plain pancake in a minute will burn the chocolate to a bitter black spot on the surface before the middle has cooked.

The batter is a classic American buttermilk mix: flour, baking powder, a little sugar, one egg, melted butter and buttermilk. The baking powder and the acid in the buttermilk produce bubbles on contact, which is why the batter should be cooked soon after mixing. Leave a few lumps. Whisking them out develops the gluten and gives flat, rubbery pancakes.

Sprinkle the chips on top of each pancake after the batter is in the pan, not into the bowl. This keeps them from sinking to the bottom and burning on the hot surface, and lets them melt into the pancake as it cooks. A heaped teaspoon per pancake is enough.

Flip when bubbles form on the surface and the edges look set, which takes about 2 minutes, and cook for 1 to 2 minutes on the second side. Keep the finished pancakes warm in a low oven and serve with butter and maple syrup.

How to Make Chocolate Chip Pancakes

Tap a step to highlight it, or turn on Cook Mode for large type and step-by-step focus.

  1. Mix the flour, sugar, baking powder and salt in a large bowl.

  2. Whisk the egg, buttermilk, melted butter and vanilla. Pour into the dry ingredients and stir until just combined, leaving a few lumps.

  3. Heat a griddle over medium-low heat and grease with a little butter. Pour 1/4 cup of batter per pancake.

  4. Scatter a heaped teaspoon of chocolate chips over each pancake. Cook for 2 minutes until bubbles form, then flip and cook for 1 to 2 minutes more.

  5. Keep warm in a low oven and serve with the maple syrup.

Worth Getting Right

  • Cook over medium-low heat to avoid burning the chips.
  • Leave a few lumps in the batter.
  • Add the chips after the batter is in the pan.

Common Substitutions & Variations

  • Out of buttermilk? Stir a tablespoon of lemon juice or vinegar into the same amount of ordinary milk and let it sit five minutes before using.

Adapting It for Your Diet

  • A measure-for-measure gluten-free flour blend can generally replace the plain flour here without changing the method, since this is savoury cooking rather than a structural bake.

Serve It With

Maple syrup · Fresh strawberries · Bacon · Whipped cream

Keeping and Reheating

Best fresh. Freeze cooked pancakes for 2 months and reheat in a toaster.
