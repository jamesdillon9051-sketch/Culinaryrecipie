Chicken Tikka Masala

Charred yogurt-marinated chicken folded into a silky, gently spiced tomato-cream sauce — the most ordered curry in Britain and one of the great crossover dishes. Grilling the chicken separately is what gives it that smoky edge.

Why This Recipe Works

Yogurt marinade works on two fronts: its lactic acid gently denatures surface proteins so the meat holds moisture, and its milk solids brown fiercely under a grill, creating the char that defines tikka. Cooking the chicken separately from the sauce means you get true dry-heat browning instead of meat poached in gravy.

Method

Tap a step to highlight it, or turn on Cook Mode for large type and step-by-step focus.

  1. Whisk all the marinade ingredients together and coat the chicken thoroughly. Cover and refrigerate for at least 2 hours, ideally overnight.

  2. Thread the chicken onto skewers, shaking off excess marinade, and grill on the highest setting 10 cm from the element for 12 to 15 minutes, turning once, until charred at the edges and cooked through.

  3. Meanwhile, heat the ghee in a heavy pan and fry the onion over medium heat for 10 to 12 minutes until deep golden. Do not rush this — pale onions make a thin sauce.

  4. Add the garlic and ginger and cook 2 minutes, then the tomato purée for 2 minutes more.

  5. Add the ground coriander, cumin, garam masala and turmeric and fry for 60 seconds, stirring constantly, until the spices darken slightly and smell toasted.

  6. Pour in the blended tomatoes, add the sugar and salt, and simmer for 15 minutes until the sauce thickens and oil beads at the surface.

  7. Blend the sauce smooth with a stick blender for a restaurant texture, then stir in the cream and warm through without boiling.

  8. Fold in the grilled chicken with any charred bits from the tray and simmer 5 minutes.

  9. Finish with chopped coriander and an extra pinch of garam masala. Serve with naan and rice.

Chef’s Tips

  • Thighs, not breasts. They stay juicy under the intense heat needed for char.
  • The oil separating from the sauce is the traditional sign that the base is properly cooked. Wait for it.
  • A teaspoon of kasuri methi (dried fenugreek leaves) crushed in at the end adds the distinctive restaurant aroma.

Pairing Suggestions

  • A off-dry Riesling or a cold lager
  • Serve with garlic naan, basmati rice and cucumber raita
  • Finish with mango kulfi

Storage & Reheating

Refrigerate 3 days, freeze 3 months. The sauce improves overnight. Reheat gently; boiling will split the cream.
