Kiwifruit Cheesecake

A no-bake cheesecake on a crushed biscuit base, with a light, creamy filling of cream cheese, whipped cream and lemon, topped with rings of fresh kiwifruit and an apricot glaze.

35 min prep and cooking plus 6 hr 30 min Chilling and setting 7 hr 5 min start to finish Serves 10 servings Medium

How to Cook Kiwifruit Cheesecake

Tap a step to highlight it, or turn on Cook Mode for large type and step-by-step focus.

  1. Grease and line a 23 cm springform tin. Crush the biscuits to fine crumbs, mix them with the melted butter, and press firmly into the base of the tin. Chill for 30 minutes.

  2. Sprinkle the gelatine over the cold water in a small bowl, and let it soak for 5 minutes, then warm it in the microwave for 20 seconds until it is clear and liquid. Let it cool slightly.

  3. Beat the cream cheese, sugar, lemon zest, lemon juice and vanilla in a large bowl until smooth.

  4. Whip the cream to soft peaks in another bowl.

  5. Stir the cooled gelatine into the cream cheese mixture, then fold in the whipped cream in two additions until smooth.

  6. Pour the filling over the base, smooth the top, and refrigerate for at least 6 hours, until firm.

  7. Warm the apricot jam with the 1 tbsp water and strain it. Just before serving, arrange the kiwifruit slices in overlapping circles on top and brush with the glaze.

  8. Run a knife around the edge, release the springform, and serve chilled.

Things to Watch For

  1. Keep the kiwifruit out of the filling. An enzyme in raw kiwifruit breaks down gelatine, so the cheesecake will not set if the fruit goes in.
  2. Add the fruit shortly before serving and glaze it. The jam seals in the juice, and because the fruit goes on late the enzyme has little time to reach the filling.
  3. Cool the melted gelatine before mixing it in. Hot gelatine can melt the cream and give a thin filling, and cold gelatine sets in strings.

The Short Version

Kiwifruit are New Zealand's best-known fruit, and a kiwifruit cheesecake is a fixture of the country's dessert tables in the winter and spring, when the fruit is at its best. The cheesecake is a no-bake one, a crushed-biscuit base holding a light filling of cream cheese, whipped cream, sugar and lemon that is set with gelatine and needs no oven. There is one rule about kiwifruit that determines the method, and it is chemical. Fresh kiwifruit contains actinidin, an enzyme that digests protein, including gelatine, so if the fruit is mixed into the filling, or even sits wet on its surface for hours, the filling will not set or will melt into a slimy layer. That is why the fruit is never in the filling, and goes on top only shortly before serving, and it is sealed with a glaze of apricot jam, which protects it and gives it a shine. The rest is straightforward, and the cheesecake sets overnight in the fridge.

What to Serve It With

A cup of tea · Fresh berries · Passionfruit pulp

Storing Leftovers

Keeps covered in the fridge for up to 3 days without the fruit. Add the kiwifruit on the day. It does not freeze well.
