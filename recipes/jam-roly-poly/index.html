Jam Roly-Poly

Suet pastry spread with jam, rolled up, and steamed into spirals. Known in every school in the country as dead man's arm, from the way it used to be boiled in a cloth.

Why This Recipe Works

Leave a clear two-centimetre border of bare pastry all the way round the jam. Jam boils and expands enormously in the steam, and a roll spread to the edges will split along its seam and empty itself into the pan. Roll it loosely too, not tightly — the pastry needs room to expand — and seal the ends by pinching them properly closed. Warm the jam slightly first so it spreads without tearing the soft pastry.

Method

Tap a step to highlight it, or turn on Cook Mode for large type and step-by-step focus.

  1. Mix the flour, suet, sugar, salt and lemon zest, then add the cold water and bring to a soft dough. Do not knead it.

  2. Roll it out on a floured surface into a rectangle about 30 by 24 cm.

  3. Warm the jam slightly with the lemon juice so it spreads easily.

  4. Spread the jam over the pastry, leaving a clear 2 cm border on every side.

  5. Brush the borders with milk and roll up loosely from a short edge, pinching the ends firmly shut.

  6. Lay it seam down on a large sheet of buttered baking paper, wrap loosely with a pleat down the middle, then wrap again in foil and twist the ends.

  7. Steam over boiling water 1 hour 45 minutes, topping up with boiling water.

  8. Unwrap, brush with milk, sprinkle with the sugar and cut into thick slices.

Chef’s Tips

  • A clear 2 cm border, or it splits and empties in the pan.
  • Roll it loosely and pleat the wrapping. It needs room.
  • Warm the jam first so it does not tear the pastry.

Pairing Suggestions

  • Hot custard
  • Cream
  • A school dining hall, ideally

Storage & Reheating

Refrigerated for 3 days. Re-steam 25 minutes.
