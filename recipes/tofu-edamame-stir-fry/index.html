Tofu and Edamame Stir-Fry

Firm tofu pressed, cornflour-crusted and fried hard, then tossed with edamame and greens in a garlic-ginger sauce. Vegan, and 42 g of protein a serving.

Where This Goes Wrong

Extra-firm tofu only. Silken and firm both collapse, and no amount of technique saves them.

Leave the tofu alone between turns. Moving it constantly is how the crust comes off in the pan.

Everything must be cut and within reach before the wok goes on. This dish takes twelve minutes and none of them are spare.

Making Tofu and Edamame Stir-Fry

Tap a step to highlight it, or turn on Cook Mode for large type and step-by-step focus.

  1. Wrap the tofu in a clean cloth, set a heavy pan on top and press it for 20 minutes. Pour away what comes out — it is a surprising amount.

  2. Cut the tofu into 2.5 cm cubes and toss with the cornflour and salt until every face is dusted.

  3. Whisk the sauce ingredients together in a small bowl and keep it beside the hob. Stir-frying leaves no time to measure.

  4. Heat 2 tablespoons of oil in a wok over high heat and fry the tofu in one layer for 6 to 8 minutes, turning every couple of minutes, until it is golden on most sides. Lift it out.

  5. Add the last tablespoon of oil and the broccoli and edamame, and stir-fry 3 minutes.

  6. Add the garlic, ginger and chilli and stir-fry 45 seconds, no longer — garlic burns and turns bitter fast at this heat.

  7. Pour in the sauce, stir until it thickens and glosses, about 30 seconds, then return the tofu and the spring onions and toss twice to coat.

Why the Method Works

Tofu is bland because it is wet, not because it is tofu. Pressing out the water and coating it in cornflour gives a surface that actually crisps, and a crisp surface holds sauce instead of shedding it. Edamame is doing quiet work here: 200 g adds around 22 g of protein, which is what takes this from a decent vegetable dish to a serious one.

Storage and Reheating

Refrigerate up to 3 days. The tofu loses its crust in the fridge and will not get it back; reheat hard in a dry pan rather than a microwave to recover some of it.

If You're Missing Something

  • Out of spring onions? A small regular onion, finely sliced, or a few chives stirred in at the end both cover the job.
  • A pinch of dried chilli flakes works in place of fresh chilli — start with about a third of the quantity the red chilli in the list implies, since dried chilli is more concentrated, and add more to taste.
  • A quarter-teaspoon of ground ginger per tablespoon of fresh is the standard ratio if you are working from the spice rack rather than a fresh root.

Adapting It for Your Diet

  • Tamari is the direct swap for light soy sauce if you need the dish gluten-free — it is brewed the same way, just without the wheat.
