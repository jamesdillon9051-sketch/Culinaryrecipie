The Idea Behind Dorada a la Sal

Compare a fish baked in a salt crust with one baked in foil and you find one that is more moist and tastes of itself. Dorada a la sal is the Spanish fish baked in a blanket of coarse salt, and the salt does not season it so much as seal it, trapping steam and juice around the flesh.

Buy a whole sea bream, scaled and gutted but with the skin on. Stuff the cavity with lemon slices, thyme and a bay leaf. Mix the coarse salt with egg whites and a splash of water until it feels like damp sand. Spread a layer of the salt on the tray, lay the fish on it and pack the rest over the top, covering the fish completely, except perhaps the tail. The crust must be sealed.

Bake at 220C for 35 minutes. Rest for 5 minutes, then crack the crust with the back of a spoon and lift off the pieces. The skin comes away with the salt, leaving white, moist flesh. Lift the fillets from the bone and serve with olive oil, lemon and boiled potatoes. The fish is not salty if the crust is cracked and removed cleanly.

Making Dorada a la Sal

Tap a step to highlight it, or turn on Cook Mode for large type and step-by-step focus.

  1. Heat the oven to 220C. Stuff the fish with the lemon slices, thyme and bay.

  2. Mix the salt, egg whites and water until it feels like damp sand.

  3. Spread a third of the salt on a tray, lay the fish on top and pack the rest over to cover it completely.

  4. Bake for 35 minutes. Rest for 5 minutes.

  5. Crack the crust, lift it away with the skin, and lift the fillets from the bone. Serve with the oil, lemon wedges and potatoes.

Notes for Getting It Right

  • Seal the fish completely in the salt.
  • Rest before cracking the crust.
  • Remove the skin with the salt.
