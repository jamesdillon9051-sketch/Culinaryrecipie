# Guide experiment plans (not yet run)

The guides in `src/data/guides.js` state only what their sources support. These comparisons would need a kitchen to settle. Nothing from them may be published until a person has run them and recorded the results here, with the date, the cook, the products used (brand, variety, pack date) and photographs.

## 1. Does rinsing change the texture of each rice? (guide: choosing-rice)

- Rices: basmati, jasmine, arborio, bomba, Japanese short-grain. One brand each, recorded.
- For each rice, two batches cooked side by side by the same method and water ratio: one rinsed until the water runs nearly clear (count the rinses), one unrinsed.
- Record: dry weight, water weight, pan (diameter, material), heat setting, time to absorption, rest time, cooked weight.
- Judge blind (a second person plates them): stickiness of grains (separate / clumping / sticky), surface starch on the spoon, photographs at the same distance and light.
- Report what was observed for these products only. Do not generalise to "rinsing does X" from one brand.

## 2. Water ratio by rice type (guide: choosing-rice)

- Same rices. For each, three ratios by weight around the packet's instruction.
- Record as above, plus whether any water was left or the base caught.

## 3. Pan-size swap for one cake (guide: pan-size)

- Recipe: `gateau-au-yaourt` (22 cm round in the recipe).
- Bake one in the stated 22 cm round tin and one in a 20 cm square tin (400 cm² against about 380 cm²).
- Record: batter depth before baking, oven (fan or not, thermometer reading), time to a clean skewer for each, rise height, photographs of the crumb.
- Only after this may the pan-size guide say anything about how much the time changed for this cake.

## 4. Split-sauce rescue with water (guide: split-sauce)

- The guide reports, as a common remedy, whisking a teaspoon of water into a sauce that is starting to look greasy. Test it on a hollandaise made with pasteurised yolks: one batch rescued with warm water, one with a fresh yolk.
- Record: temperature of the bowl when it started to split (thermometer), amount of water, whether it came back, time taken.
