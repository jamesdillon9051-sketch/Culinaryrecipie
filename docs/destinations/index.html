Best places to visit in France

Alpine summits, Atlantic oyster beds, Roman ruins and the world's most visited city — inside one very well-connected country.

1
Major CityCapital City

Paris

Île-de-France

France's capital packs more world-class museums, monuments and neighbourhood restaurants into six square miles than anywhere else in Europe. It rewards walking more than ticking off a list.

Don't miss The Louvre, the Musée d'Orsay and Sainte-Chapelle — three of Europe's great collections within one Métro ride of each other.

2
Scenic RegionFood & Wine

Provence & the Luberon

Vaucluse, Provence-Alpes-Côte d'Azur

A landscape of ochre cliffs, perched stone villages and vineyards between the Rhône and the Alps, with the best-preserved Roman monuments outside Italy on its western edge.

Don't miss The Pont du Gard, a three-tier Roman aqueduct that has stood for two thousand years without mortar.

Explore Destination → — Provence & the Luberon
3
Coast & BeachesMajor City

Nice & the French Riviera

Alpes-Maritimes, Provence-Alpes-Côte d'Azur

France's fifth city curves around a two-mile bay of blue water, with an old town that was Italian until 1860 and a local rail line linking a dozen coastal villages.

Don't miss The Cours Saleya flower market, then the coastal train to Villefranche, Èze and Menton for €2 a hop.

Explore Destination → — Nice & the French Riviera
4
Historic SiteUNESCO Site

The Loire Valley

Centre-Val de Loire

France's royal playground for two centuries: Renaissance châteaux, formal gardens and crisp white wine strung along 170 miles of the country's longest river, an hour from Paris.

Don't miss Chenonceau, arched on five spans across the River Cher — the most beautiful building in France by most reckonings.

Explore Destination → — The Loire Valley
5
UNESCO SiteHistoric Site

Mont-Saint-Michel

Manche, Normandy

A rock in a vast tidal bay, crowned by an abbey that took five centuries to build. Some three million people visit a year, and almost all of them leave before the best hours.

Don't miss The abbey after the day-trippers leave, when the village empties and the bay goes silent.

Explore Destination → — Mont-Saint-Michel
6
MountainsNature & Wildlife

Chamonix & Mont Blanc

Haute-Savoie, Auvergne-Rhône-Alpes

A valley town beneath a 4,808-metre summit, where glaciers reach almost to the treeline and a network of lifts puts high-alpine terrain within reach of anyone.

Don't miss The Aiguille du Midi: a rock spire with a viewing terrace at 3,842 m, reached by the highest vertical-ascent cable car in the world.

Explore Destination → — Chamonix & Mont Blanc
7
Major CityFood & Wine

Bordeaux

Gironde, Nouvelle-Aquitaine

An 18th-century city of pale limestone facades along a curving river, transformed over two decades from soot-blackened port to one of France's most liveable cities.

Don't miss The Miroir d'Eau opposite the Place de la Bourse: the world's largest reflecting pool, and the best free show in France.

Explore Destination → — Bordeaux
8
Historic SiteCoast & Beaches

Normandy & the D-Day Beaches

Calvados and Manche, Normandy

Five beaches, a dozen museums, cliff-top batteries and immaculately kept cemeteries, in a green farming region that also gave the world Camembert, cider and Calvados.

Don't miss The Normandy American Cemetery above Omaha Beach — 9,388 graves in white marble, looking down on the sand.

Explore Destination → — Normandy & the D-Day Beaches
9
Culture & ArtsUNESCO Site

Strasbourg & Alsace

Bas-Rhin and Haut-Rhin, Grand Est

A region that changed nationality four times in seventy-five years and kept both cultures — French administration, German architecture, and a food and wine tradition that belongs to neither.

Don't miss Strasbourg Cathedral's astronomical clock and the 142-metre spire you can climb for a view into three countries.

Explore Destination → — Strasbourg & Alsace
10
Historic SiteNature & Wildlife

The Dordogne & Sarlat

Dordogne, Nouvelle-Aquitaine

A limestone valley of castles, walnut groves and honey-coloured villages, holding the densest concentration of prehistoric sites in Europe and France's richest regional cooking.

Don't miss Lascaux — the most sophisticated prehistoric art ever found, seen through a replica so exact you forget it is one.
